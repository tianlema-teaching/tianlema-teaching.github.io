How to use the slides

Each lecture is a single page that runs in your browser. It needs no installation or account, and it loads nothing from other sites.

  • Slides opens the presentation. Use the right arrow or Space to go forward, and the left arrow to go back. N shows the spoken explanation for the current slide. Tab shows every slide, F switches to full screen, I switches between light and dark, and ? lists every key.
  • Study guide opens the same lecture as one long page with every step shown. To keep a copy, print it and choose "Save as PDF". The spoken explanations are not on this page; open the slides and press N to read them.
  • Quiz slides reveal the answer after you choose an option. Nothing is recorded or graded.

Before you reveal a step, predict it. Pause on each pseudocode slide and trace the lines on paper. The Java code is written for these slides and tested. Your assignments may ask for different method names or behavior, so always follow the assignment.

Limits

  • The slides are drafts. They have been checked for errors, but they have not yet had an independent human review. Enrolled students can report a mistake through the usual course channels.
  • Slides are designed for a laptop or projector. On a phone, the study guide is easier to read than the presentation.
  • Slide text and code are text that a screen reader can read. The diagrams are drawn as unlabeled images; their meaning is given in each slide's spoken explanation (N). The slides have not been reviewed for screen-reader use.
  • An animation or a trace shows one example input. It is not a proof of correctness or running time. The proof sketches and cost slides give those arguments.
